\begin{abstract}
Jianpu specifies musical pitches and rhythm, whereas jianzipu describes the actions used to perform them on the guqin. Generating jianzipu requires selecting pitch-correct, physically feasible actions that follow playing conventions and support the instrument's characteristic expression, or \emph{yunwei}. These choices depend on tuning, inherited notation state, and the surrounding phrase. We introduce \system, a framework for generating jianzipu from complete jianpu scores through tool interaction. We provide a full-score benchmark and executable runtime, a two-stage agent that separates basic fingering from idiomatic refinement, and an expert-guided inverse trajectory construction method for training a student from annotated scores. Runtime tools expose pitch candidates, editable plans, and conservative warnings to support revision. Evaluation combines pitch correctness, structured action agreement, technique distributions, and task completion; performer assessment is planned to address qualities beyond automatic checks. On 48 scores, the two-stage full system achieves 99.981\% pitch accuracy within 50 cents on 15,758 auditor-resolvable events. A single-stage student reaches 98.732\% but has higher reference fingering and ornament agreement, revealing a trade-off between pitch consistency and reference action agreement. We will release the model, redacted training trajectories, benchmark, and runtime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A melody specifies which notes to play, but realizing it on the guqin also requires deciding how to produce and connect them. Jianzipu records these performance actions through combinations of strings, hui positions, left-hand fingers, right-hand strokes, and techniques \cite{li2026zeroshotjianzi,li2026jztab}. Preparing a jianzipu realization from a jianpu score therefore calls for instrument-specific judgment. A system that proposes editable realizations and exposes detectable errors could support performers and learners in arranging melodies for the guqin.

The goal involves four related requirements: pitch fidelity, physical playability, adherence to playing conventions, and idiomatic musical expression. Several string--position combinations may produce the same pitch, yet their hand shapes, transitions, and tonal effects can differ. Two individually valid notes may be difficult to execute together or connect naturally. Suitable choices of slides, vibrato, and compound gestures also contribute to \emph{yunwei}, the guqin's characteristic expressive quality. Adding more ornaments alone does not establish this quality; their placement and relation to the phrase matter.

These requirements create three challenges. First, generation involves choosing among multiple realizations under both physical and musical constraints. Pitch agreement does not establish whether a hand position is feasible, whether a stroke follows conventional usage, or whether a technique suits the phrase. Second, decisions depend on the evolving state of the complete score. A harmonic region remains active until terminated, and subsequent notation may omit positions inherited from earlier events. The same string and hui can therefore imply different pitches inside and outside a harmonic region. Local edits must be interpreted in context and may change the meaning of later entries.

Third, expert annotations provide final realizations but generally omit the process of choosing, checking, and revising them. Final notation alone does not demonstrate when an agent should query pitch candidates, inspect preceding material, or respond to a warning. Recent jianzipu research addresses glyph recognition and structured extraction from manuscript pages \cite{li2026zeroshotjianzi,li2026jztab}. Here, the input is a complete machine-readable jianpu score, and the task is to construct a guqin performance realization. This setting connects symbolic music generation with planning actions whose interpretation and feasibility depend on prior choices.

We address these challenges through interactive plan editing. Following work on reasoning with tools and iterative feedback \cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,madaan2023selfrefine}, \system partitions a complete score into manageable work units while maintaining one shared notation plan. Its runtime provides tuning-aware candidates and conservative checks for pitch, state, and supported performance rules. A Fingering stage establishes a basic realization; a Guqinizer stage considers idiomatic technique and continuity. An offline teacher uses private expert annotations to construct actual tool interactions, which are redacted into student-visible supervision. Automatic checks support the verifiable parts of the task, while performer assessment is needed to judge expressive suitability. In summary, our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item We introduce \emph{constrained jianzipu generation}, a benchmark and executable runtime for reproducible score-to-jianzipu evaluation.
\item We introduce warning-guided inference: deterministic pitch and conservative notation-constraint diagnostics become visible, actionable feedback rather than hidden post-hoc validation.
\item We design \system, a two-stage Fingering$\rightarrow$Guqinizer agent that distinguishes basic sound production from stylistic refinement.
\item We develop targeted inverse trajectory construction, which replays annotations through the runtime and exports public, redacted supervision.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Constrained Generation with an Executable Runtime}\label{sec:formulation}
Given a complete jianpu score $x=(m,e_{1:N})$, the task is to construct an event-indexed jianzipu plan $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_N)$. The metadata $m$ specifies the mode and tuning; each event $e_i$ has a stable index, notated duration, structural type, and a target pitch $q_i$ when defined. An output entry $y_i$ is either a jianzipu expression or an explicit empty display value. The latter may indicate continuation or coverage by a compound gesture and does not remove the event from the score. ABC notation is used only as an internal representation for pitch processing, not as an additional input.

Unlike independent token prediction, the interpretation of $y_i$ depends on preceding actions. We represent this dependence by a deterministic notation-state transition,
\begin{equation}
    s_i=\mathcal{T}(s_{i-1},y_i;m),\qquad s_0=s_{\mathrm{init}}(m),
    \label{eq:notation-state}
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ records such properties as harmonic-region status, inherited left-hand position, walking gestures, and compound-action coverage. Given this state, the realized pitch $\hat q_i=\mathcal{P}(y_i,s_{i-1};m)$ is a partial function: some compound or ambiguous expressions do not admit a reliable pitch estimate. Structural consistency and pitch agreement are therefore distinct from the broader question of musical suitability.

\paragraph{Interactive plan editing.}
The complete score is partitioned into an ordered sequence of bounded, musically aligned work units $\mathcal{B}(x)=(p_1,\ldots,p_K)$. This partition is computed from the input score; the benchmark examples remain complete scores. The agent edits one shared plan rather than generating independent phrases. At interaction step $t$ within $p_k$, its public history consists of the score, current work unit, editable plan, replayed notation state, and previous tool observations:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        h_t&=(x,p_k,y^{(t)},s^{(t)},o_{<t}),\\
        a_t&\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid h_t,r),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $r$ denotes the current planning stage and $s^{(t)}$ is obtained by replaying the current plan. The executable runtime $\mathcal{R}$ processes an action and returns the resulting plan, state, and tool observation:
\begin{equation}
    (y^{(t+1)},s^{(t+1)},o_t)
    =\mathcal{R}(x,y^{(t)},s^{(t)},a_t).
    \label{eq:runtime}
\end{equation}
Read-only actions leave the plan unchanged. The runtime used to construct training trajectories is also used at inference time.

The agent can request tuning-aware string--hui realizations through \texttt{get\_pitch\_candidates}, including open strings, stopped notes, harmonics, and supported inherited or compound gestures. Returned candidates distinguish exact, approximate, and unavailable matches. The only plan-writing operation is \texttt{edit\_plan(jianzi\_rows)}, which applies event-indexed updates and replays the resulting notation state. Compact context retrieval through \texttt{list\_context} and targeted earlier-phrase lookup provides prior material without reproducing the entire score in every interaction.

\paragraph{Conservative constraint feedback.}
We distinguish runtime-checkable violations from cases whose interpretation is uncertain. For a rule $c$ and event $i$, let $v_c(i;x,y,s)\in\{0,1,\bot\}$ denote no detected violation, a detected violation, or an indeterminate check, respectively. The warning set is
\begin{equation}
    W(x,y)=\{(i,c):v_c(i;x,y,s)=1\},
    \label{eq:warnings}
\end{equation}
with $s$ obtained by replaying $y$. In the implementation, checks cover reliably parsed pitch mismatches and conservative diagnostics for harmonic-state continuity, walk direction or span, interrupted open-string resonance, paired stroke adjacency, and difficult left-hand reaches. Indeterminate cases are not converted into violations. Warnings accompany the relevant \texttt{edit\_plan} preview but neither rewrite the plan nor imply that an unflagged output is musically correct. The agent may query candidates, revise the affected entries, or retain its choice; identical repeated edits are stopped to prevent unproductive loops.

\subsection{Two-Stage Performance-Action Planning}\label{sec:two-stage}
The same notation event jointly specifies how a pitch is produced and how it is articulated. We separate these decisions into two successive planning stages. \emph{Fingering} first constructs a basic pitch-grounded realization, choosing strings, hui positions, left-hand contact, and elementary right-hand strokes. \emph{Guqinizer} then revises the committed plan for idiomatic execution, including ornaments, walking gestures, harmonic transitions, and compound-action notation. The second stage may preserve or revise earlier decisions; it is not a post-processing formatter.

For work unit $p_k$, let $y^{(k-1)}$ be the shared plan after completing the preceding units. A bounded runtime rollout under each stage gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    y^{F,(k)}&=\operatorname{Rollout}_{F}(x,p_k,y^{(k-1)};\mathcal{R}),\\
    y^{(k)}&=\operatorname{Rollout}_{G}(x,p_k,y^{F,(k)};\mathcal{R}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stage-composition}
\end{equation}
where each rollout consists of stage-conditioned tool interactions of the form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:runtime}. Only edits committed through the runtime enter the shared plan. Crucially, the state passed to $p_{k+1}$ is replayed from $y^{(k)}$, including any earlier mistakes, rather than substituted from an expert reference. Both stages retain access to pitch candidates and runtime feedback. Warnings can trigger another revision but are not hard acceptance criteria; execution remains subject to the configured interaction budget.

This decomposition imposes a procedural bias: establish a basic sound-producing realization before making the more context-dependent choices involved in idiomatic execution. It does not assume a unique separation between fingering and ornamentation, nor does it guarantee that the second stage preserves every correct decision. We evaluate both the benefits and regressions introduced by refinement against a single-stage agent using the same public tools.

\subsection{Expert-Guided Inverse Trajectory Learning}\label{sec:inverse}
Expert jianzipu annotations specify desired performance actions but generally not the tool interactions by which an agent could arrive at them. Directly supervising final notation therefore leaves the interaction policy unspecified. We instead construct trajectories by allowing an offline teacher to work backward from the annotated target while acting through the same executable runtime available to the student.

Let $z$ be a phrase-level expert annotation and $\mathcal{K}(z)$ the technique-relevant descriptions retrieved from a curated knowledge base. Both are private to the teacher. Its rollout is conditioned on the input, private guidance, and public runtime:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^T\sim\operatorname{Rollout}
      \bigl(\pi_T\mid x,z,\mathcal{K}(z);\mathcal{R}\bigr),
    \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
where $\tau^T$ records the chronological sequence of tool actions, returned observations, and accepted plan updates. The reference guides the search for an executable realization but does not constitute a tool response. The teacher constructs stage-specific traces: basic fingering first, followed by idiomatic refinement. Semantic descriptions clarify technique constraints that may be implicit in compact jianzipu symbols; they are not added to the student's inference context.

\paragraph{Validation and public-prefix reconstruction.}
Candidate examples are screened for reference parsing and consistency before construction, and generated traces are checked against the runtime. Training examples are obtained by removing private expert content and retaining the public interaction sequence,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \tilde\tau&=g_{\mathrm{pub}}(\tau^T),\\
        \mathcal{D}&=\{\tilde\tau:\tau^T\text{ passes validation}\}.
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:public-trajectory}
\end{equation}
The intended information boundary is temporal as well as structural: at each supervised action, the conditioning context should contain only the input, earlier public actions, and observations already returned by the runtime. Removing private fields alone cannot establish this property if rewritten reasoning anticipates future observations or reproduces unseen annotation details. Public-prefix consistency must therefore be checked separately from runtime execution validity; the precise checks and filtering counts belong in the experimental protocol.

\paragraph{Student supervision.}
We fit a stage-conditioned student policy to the assistant-side actions and messages in the retained public trajectories. For a supervised token position $u$, let $h_u^{\mathrm{pub}}$ contain the preceding student-visible context and let $w_u$ be its target token. The training loss is standard masked cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)
    =-\sum_{\tilde\tau\in\mathcal{D}}
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal{Y}(\tilde\tau)}
      \log p_\theta(w_u\mid h_u^{\mathrm{pub}}),
    \label{eq:sft}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Y}(\tilde\tau)$ indexes supervised assistant tokens; tool outputs form context but are not prediction targets. At deployment, neither expert annotations nor private semantic guidance are provided. The resulting student must recover its actions from the input score, its own previous edits, and the executable feedback it has observed.

\section{Conclusion}
\system frames jianzipu generation as executable, stateful plan editing. Its benchmark makes score events, tuning, and notation state explicit; its two-stage architecture separates basic realization from idiomatic refinement; and its inverse trajectories create public tool-use supervision from private annotations. On 48 scores, both trained students complete all 1,031 work units. The two-stage full system attains higher auditor-resolvable pitch accuracy and fewer final warnings, while the single-stage student achieves higher reference agreement across fingering, tone type, and ornaments. These complementary outcomes motivate evaluating execution quality and musical expression alongside pitch consistency. The fixed-workflow trajectory baseline attains closer aggregate technique usage but much lower pitch accuracy than the standard single-stage student, underscoring the distinction between reproducing action patterns and realizing the input melody. The GLM-based GEPA comparison and performer assessment remain to be completed.

\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Resource Availability}
The auditor is conservative and incomplete: no warning is not proof of musical correctness, and unresolved notation is not a success. Fine-grained left-hand reachability, timbre, and lineage-specific style still require expert review or additional physical modeling. The digitized score collection may not represent all historical sources or playing traditions. We position \system as an assistive research system, not a replacement for performers or tradition bearers. Before release, we will review source licenses and provenance, remove private teacher material, and publish only redacted trajectories intended for public supervision. Since inference feedback and final warning scoring share domain rules, warning reductions are not independent evidence of performer-rated quality. Model-dependent parseability changes the pitch denominator even with a fixed input score. The stage comparison uses different retry and pass structures, so nominal round ceilings do not establish equal total inference budgets. The original-model agent's low output coverage also prevents a full-test musical-quality comparison. The trajectory-construction comparison also includes differences in training prompts and trajectory lengths, limiting causal attribution to individual construction steps. GEPA and expert evaluation remain pending.

\paragraph{Open Science and Resource Availability.}
We will release the trained model, redacted training trajectories, the test benchmark, and the executable runtime used by teacher generation and inference. The release will include score-level split definitions, event-indexed schemas, evaluation scripts, filtering manifests, and instructions for reproducing the benchmark. Private teacher prompts, unredacted teacher reasoning, and any source material that cannot be redistributed will not be included.
