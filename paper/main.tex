\begin{abstract}
The guqin is a traditional Chinese seven-string zither. Jianpu records pitches and rhythm numerically; jianzipu combines character components to encode performance actions. Jianpu-to-jianzipu generation is thus stateful, constrained performance-action planning rather than direct symbolic transcription. We introduce \system, combining tool-interactive planning, domain-specific fine-tuning, and expert-guided inverse trajectory learning. A teacher uses expert scores to construct executable interactions, redacted into public supervision for single-stage and two-stage agents. The full two-stage system separates basic fingering from idiomatic refinement; both variants use pitch candidates, editable plans, and diagnostic feedback. On 48 scores, the full system achieves 99.981\% pitch accuracy within 50 cents on auditor-resolvable events, compared with 86.237\% without diagnostic feedback. Our single-stage variant achieves 98.732\% and higher reference fingering and ornament agreement. Modern-melody results further show that conditional pitch accuracy and verified-correct output coverage can favor different variants. Evaluation therefore combines pitch fidelity, coverage, reference-action agreement, and runtime diagnostics, with performer assessment planned for feasibility and musical expression. We will release the model, redacted training trajectories, benchmark, and runtime.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The guqin is a traditional Chinese seven-string zither whose jianzipu notation records performance actions rather than pitches alone. Individual jianzi combine abbreviated Chinese character components to encode string numbers, hui positions along the strings, left- and right-hand fingering, and performance techniques \cite{li2026zeroshotjianzi,li2026jztab}. Jianpu uses numbers and rhythmic marks to specify a melody's pitches and durations. Our input is thus a melody in modern notation, written in numbered notation and expressible in Western staff notation with the same pitches and rhythms.

Realizing a jianpu melody on the guqin requires choosing among alternative string--hui positions and techniques for the same pitch. These choices affect hand configuration, transitions, and timbre; individually correct actions need not form a natural, connected phrase. We therefore frame jianpu-to-jianzipu generation as stateful, constrained performance-action planning rather than direct symbolic transcription. Pitch fidelity and consistency with implemented runtime rules provide checkable evidence, but neither certifies physical playability or idiomatic quality. Slides, vibrato, and compound gestures contribute to \emph{yunwei}, yet more ornaments alone do not imply more expressive playing.

Figure~\ref{fig:one-pitch} aligns five input notes from \emph{Ban Hu Sha} with their generated actions. For the second note, the runtime flags the proposed 702-cent slide from hui 7 to hui 5. The agent revises it to an outward pluck at hui 5 on the same string. The destination stays the same while the transition changes.

Three challenges follow. \emph{Context-Constrained Performance Planning.} A pitch admits multiple realizations, but selecting one requires jointly considering performance constraints, historical notation state, and phrase context. Harmonic regions and inherited hand positions affect how later entries sound; a locally pitch-correct action need not fit the phrase or preserve subsequent consistency.

\emph{Domain-Specific Musical Knowledge Gap.} General-purpose LLMs may know technique names and verbal definitions without reliably grounding them in musical meaning, execution, or appropriate use. Such terminology familiarity cannot be assumed to transfer to context-sensitive action selection, motivating domain-specific training rather than reliance on instructions alone.

\emph{Missing Process Supervision.} Expert jianzipu annotations specify final realizations but generally omit the decisions used to select, verify, and revise actions. Final notation alone does not demonstrate when to query pitch candidates, inspect preceding material, or respond to a warning; these interactions are not directly supervised by final artifacts.

To support context-constrained planning, \system combines two-stage decomposition with interactive plan editing, following work on reasoning with tools and iterative feedback \cite{yao2023react,schick2023toolformer,madaan2023selfrefine}. It partitions a complete score into manageable work units while maintaining one shared notation plan. The runtime supplies tuning-aware candidates and conservative checks for pitch, state, and supported performance rules. A Fingering stage establishes a basic realization; a Guqinizer stage considers idiomatic technique and continuity. For domain-specific action learning and process supervision, we fine-tune students on expert-guided inverse trajectories: an offline teacher uses private expert annotations to execute actual tool interactions, which are redacted into student-visible supervision. Automatic checks address verifiable properties, while performer assessment remains necessary for expressive suitability. In summary, our contributions are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item We introduce \emph{constrained jianzipu generation}, a benchmark and executable runtime for reproducible score-to-jianzipu evaluation.
\item We introduce warning-guided inference: deterministic pitch and notation diagnostics are exposed as feedback during generation, enabling the agent to revise its plan.
\item We design \system, with a single-stage trajectory-trained variant and a full two-stage Fingering$\rightarrow$Guqinizer agent that distinguishes basic sound production from stylistic refinement. Their comparison measures pitch consistency and reference-action agreement; performer evaluation addresses execution and expression.
\item We develop targeted inverse trajectory construction, which replays annotations through the runtime and exports public, redacted supervision.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Constrained Generation with an Executable Runtime}\label{sec:formulation}
Given a complete jianpu score $x=(m,e_{1:N})$, the task is to construct an event-indexed jianzipu plan $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_N)$. The metadata $m$ specifies the mode and tuning; each event $e_i$ has a stable index, notated duration, structural type, and a target pitch $q_i$ when defined. An output entry $y_i$ is either a jianzipu expression or an explicit empty display value. The latter may indicate continuation or coverage by a compound gesture and does not remove the event from the score. ABC notation is used only as an internal representation for pitch processing, not as an additional input.

Unlike independent token prediction, the interpretation of $y_i$ depends on preceding actions. We represent this dependence by a deterministic notation-state transition,
\begin{equation}
    s_i=\mathcal{T}(s_{i-1},y_i;m),\qquad s_0=s_{\mathrm{init}}(m),
    \label{eq:notation-state}
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ records such properties as harmonic-region status, inherited left-hand position, walking gestures, and compound-action coverage. Given this state, the realized pitch $\hat q_i=\mathcal{P}(y_i,s_{i-1};m)$ is a partial function: some compound or ambiguous expressions do not admit a reliable pitch estimate. Structural consistency and pitch agreement are therefore distinct from the broader question of musical suitability.

\paragraph{Interactive plan editing.}
The complete score is partitioned into an ordered sequence of bounded, musically aligned work units $\mathcal{B}(x)=(p_1,\ldots,p_K)$. This partition is computed from the input score; the benchmark examples remain complete scores. The agent edits one shared plan rather than generating independent phrases. At interaction step $t$ within $p_k$, its public history consists of the score, current work unit, editable plan, replayed notation state, and previous tool observations:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        h_t&=(x,p_k,y^{(t)},s^{(t)},o_{<t}),\\
        a_t&\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid h_t,r),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $r$ denotes the current planning stage and $s^{(t)}$ is obtained by replaying the current plan. The executable runtime $\mathcal{R}$ processes an action and returns the resulting plan, state, and tool observation:
\begin{equation}
    (y^{(t+1)},s^{(t+1)},o_t)
    =\mathcal{R}(x,y^{(t)},s^{(t)},a_t).
    \label{eq:runtime}
\end{equation}
Read-only actions leave the plan unchanged. The runtime used to construct training trajectories is also used at inference time.

The agent can request tuning-aware string--hui realizations through \texttt{get\_pitch\_candidates}, including open strings, stopped notes, harmonics, and supported inherited or compound gestures. Returned candidates distinguish exact, approximate, and unavailable matches. The only plan-writing operation is \texttt{edit\_plan(jianzi\_rows)}, which applies event-indexed updates and replays the resulting notation state. Compact context retrieval through \texttt{list\_context} and targeted earlier-phrase lookup provides prior material without reproducing the entire score in every interaction.

\paragraph{Conservative constraint feedback.}
We distinguish runtime-checkable violations from cases whose interpretation is uncertain. For a rule $c$ and event $i$, let $v_c(i;x,y,s)\in\{0,1,\bot\}$ denote no detected violation, a detected violation, or an indeterminate check, respectively. The warning set is
\begin{equation}
    W(x,y)=\{(i,c):v_c(i;x,y,s)=1\},
    \label{eq:warnings}
\end{equation}
with $s$ obtained by replaying $y$. In the implementation, checks cover reliably parsed pitch mismatches and conservative diagnostics for harmonic-state continuity, walk direction or span, interrupted open-string resonance, paired stroke adjacency, and difficult left-hand reaches. Indeterminate cases are not converted into violations. Warnings accompany the relevant \texttt{edit\_plan} preview but neither rewrite the plan nor imply that an unflagged output is musically correct. The agent may query candidates, revise the affected entries, or retain its choice; identical repeated edits are stopped to prevent unproductive loops.

\subsection{Two-Stage Performance-Action Planning}\label{sec:two-stage}
The same notation event jointly specifies how a pitch is produced and how it is articulated. We separate these decisions into two successive planning stages. \emph{Fingering} first constructs a basic pitch-grounded realization, choosing strings, hui positions, left-hand contact, and elementary right-hand strokes. \emph{Guqinizer} then revises the committed plan for idiomatic execution, including ornaments, walking gestures, harmonic transitions, and compound-action notation. The second stage may preserve or revise earlier decisions; it is not a post-processing formatter.

For work unit $p_k$, let $y^{(k-1)}$ be the shared plan after completing the preceding units. A bounded runtime rollout under each stage gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    y^{F,(k)}&=\operatorname{Rollout}_{F}(x,p_k,y^{(k-1)};\mathcal{R}),\\
    y^{(k)}&=\operatorname{Rollout}_{G}(x,p_k,y^{F,(k)};\mathcal{R}),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stage-composition}
\end{equation}
where each rollout consists of stage-conditioned tool interactions of the form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:runtime}. Only edits committed through the runtime enter the shared plan. Crucially, the state passed to $p_{k+1}$ is replayed from $y^{(k)}$, including any earlier mistakes, rather than substituted from an expert reference. Both stages retain access to pitch candidates and runtime feedback. Warnings can trigger another revision but are not hard acceptance criteria; execution remains subject to the configured interaction budget.

This decomposition imposes a procedural bias: establish a basic sound-producing realization before making the more context-dependent choices involved in idiomatic execution. It does not assume a unique separation between fingering and ornamentation, nor does it guarantee that the second stage preserves every correct decision. We evaluate both the benefits and regressions introduced by refinement against a single-stage agent using the same public tools.

\subsection{Expert-Guided Inverse Trajectory Learning}\label{sec:inverse}
Expert jianzipu annotations specify desired performance actions but generally not the tool interactions by which an agent could arrive at them. Directly supervising final notation therefore leaves the interaction policy unspecified. We instead construct trajectories by allowing an offline teacher to work backward from the annotated target while acting through the same executable runtime available to the student.

Let $z$ be a phrase-level expert annotation and $\mathcal{K}(z)$ the technique-relevant descriptions retrieved from a curated knowledge base. Both are private to the teacher. Its rollout is conditioned on the input, private guidance, and public runtime:
\begin{equation}
    \tau^T\sim\operatorname{Rollout}
      \bigl(\pi_T\mid x,z,\mathcal{K}(z);\mathcal{R}\bigr),
    \label{eq:teacher}
\end{equation}
where $\tau^T$ records the chronological sequence of tool actions, returned observations, and accepted plan updates. The reference guides the search for an executable realization but does not constitute a tool response. The teacher constructs stage-specific traces: basic fingering first, followed by idiomatic refinement. Semantic descriptions clarify technique constraints that may be implicit in compact jianzipu symbols; they are not added to the student's inference context.

\paragraph{Student supervision.}
We remove private expert guidance from the teacher traces and fit a stage-conditioned student policy to the retained public interaction sequences $\mathcal{D}$. For a supervised token position $u$ in a trajectory $\tilde\tau\in\mathcal{D}$, let $h_u^{\mathrm{pub}}$ contain the preceding student-visible context and let $w_u$ be its target token. The training loss is standard masked cross-entropy,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SFT}}(\theta)
    =-\sum_{\tilde\tau\in\mathcal{D}}
      \sum_{u\in\mathcal{Y}(\tilde\tau)}
      \log p_\theta(w_u\mid h_u^{\mathrm{pub}}),
    \label{eq:sft}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{Y}(\tilde\tau)$ indexes supervised assistant tokens; tool outputs form context but are not prediction targets. At deployment, neither expert annotations nor private semantic guidance are provided. The resulting student must recover its actions from the input score, its own previous edits, and the executable feedback it has observed.

\section{Conclusion}
\system frames jianzipu generation as executable, stateful plan editing. Its inverse trajectory construction turns private expert annotations into public tool-use supervision, supporting both single-stage generation and a two-stage pipeline for basic realization and idiomatic refinement. Both variants complete all 1,031 work units in the 48-score test. The full system achieves 99.981\% pitch accuracy on auditor-resolvable events and leaves two runtime warnings, while our single-stage variant achieves higher reference agreement across fingering, tone type, and ornaments. On modern melodies, the full system also has higher conditional pitch accuracy, whereas single-stage generation resolves more target events and yields more verified-correct events overall. The fixed-workflow comparison shows that reproducing reference technique patterns alone does not ensure pitch consistency. Together, these results distinguish pitch fidelity, coverage, and reference agreement as complementary aspects of performance realization.

\section{Limitations, Ethics, and Resource Availability}
The auditor is conservative and incomplete. Fine-grained left-hand reachability, timbre, and lineage-specific style require performer assessment or additional physical modeling. Inference feedback and final scoring share domain rules. Lower violation rates indicate closer adherence to the encoded playing practices. Independent performer assessment is needed to judge the resulting phrasing and expression. Generated notation also affects pitch resolvability; conditional accuracy must be read alongside coverage. The comparisons use different interaction budgets and trajectory-construction recipes, as specified in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}, and do not isolate every component's causal effect. The independence of the modern melodies from all development procedures remains to be verified. The digitized collection may not represent all historical sources or playing traditions. \system is intended to support performers and learners in preparing editable realizations. Performer evaluation remains pending (Appendix~\ref{sec:expert-protocol}), as does the GEPA comparison.

\paragraph{Open Science and Resource Availability.}
We will release the trained model, redacted training trajectories, the test benchmark, and the executable runtime used by teacher generation and inference. The release will include score-level split definitions, event-indexed schemas, evaluation scripts, filtering manifests, and instructions for reproducing the benchmark. Private teacher prompts, unredacted teacher reasoning, and any source material that cannot be redistributed will not be included.
