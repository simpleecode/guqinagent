All outputs are evaluated using the same current rules after replaying each complete score in chronological order. This preserves harmonic regions, left-hand positions, and other state across work-unit boundaries. Runtime snapshots and evaluation code fingerprints are retained for auditability.

\paragraph{Student Training and Inference Budgets.}
The training split contains 153 scores and 3,976 work units. Two-stage supervision yields 7,952 stage-specific conversations, jointly training one adapter used by both stages; single-stage trajectory supervision, fixed-workflow supervision, and Direct SFT each yield 3,976 examples. All four students start from Qwen3.5-9B and use 4-bit LoRA fine-tuning for three epochs, with rank 8, scaling $\alpha=16$, learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, effective batch size 12, and an 8,192-token training cutoff. Equal epochs do not imply equal numbers of conversations or supervised tokens. Standard preprocessing truncation applies at the cutoff; counting a conversation does not certify that every original token is supervised.

At inference, local Qwen conditions use a 16,384-token context and at most 8,192 output tokens per request. Tool-based single-stage conditions allow up to 16 rounds per attempt; two-stage conditions allow eight rounds per stage attempt, with at most three attempts and, when feedback is enabled, three Guqinizer passes. These are ceilings, not required numbers of interactions. Feedback OFF performs one Guqinizer pass. Direct generates once per work unit, without tools or repair. Thus these are deployed-pipeline comparisons, not equal-total-token experiments. Full configurations, sampling settings, and dataset/checkpoint fingerprints appear in Appendix~\ref{sec:reproducibility}.

\paragraph{Evaluation Metrics.}
Pitch correctness is measured against the input jianpu melody. The expert jianzipu annotation supplies reference actions for fingering and technique metrics. Let $\mathcal I$ contain events for which the auditor returns a determinate matched or mismatched prediction. Let $a_i=1$ if event $i$ matches the target within 50 cents under the supported matching rules, and $a_i=0$ otherwise. Let $\mathcal A_i$ contain the tone pairs aligned by the auditor, with absolute error $\delta_{ij}$ in cents for pair $j$. We report
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{PitchAcc}_{50}
&=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}a_i}{|\mathcal I|},\\
\mathrm{PitchMAE}
&=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}\sum_{j\in\mathcal A_i}\delta_{ij}}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I}|\mathcal A_i|}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pitch-metrics}
\end{equation}
Accuracy uses events as its denominator; MAE averages aligned tone pairs, and a compound event can contribute multiple pairs. Unresolved cases are excluded from pitch scoring and must be reported separately. We report each condition's evaluable event count and accuracy on their intersection, since generated notation affects which expressions the auditor resolves. The complete event inventory includes structural and continuation events and is not the pitch denominator.

String--Hui F1 is a micro set F1 over exact $(\text{string},\text{hui})$ pairs within each event, using the stateful auditor's resolved positions. All resolved positions of a compound event are included, without imposing a primary/secondary ordering. Open strings receive an explicit OPEN contact label; numeric hui positions are normalized to six decimal places. Events with unresolved reference positions are excluded, whereas unresolved predictions at scorable reference events contribute missing pairs. The common reference coverage is 12,327/18,128 target events (68.000\%) for traditional scores and 1,280/1,528 (83.770\%) for modern melodies; the reference contains 12,821 and 1,480 scorable position pairs, respectively. This is conditional reference-position agreement, not a complete transcription score. Left-hand fingering, right-hand fingering, and tone type are reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:detailed-results}. Tone type distinguishes open, stopped, and harmonic notes. Ornament precision, recall, and F1 compare event-level sets of named embellishment and compound actions. They share the technique-distribution lexicon and alias normalization below, but exclude the eight basic plucks (mo, tiao, gou, ti, da, zhai, tuo, and bo). Known compounds remain atomic labels. Harmonic-region start/end markers are retained only as state controls, never as techniques or ornaments. A nonempty disjoint label set scores zero F1; two empty corpus sets are undefined. These metrics measure agreement with one expert realization, not correctness of execution.

Technique distribution similarity summarizes the relative use of named performance techniques. Each technique is counted at most once per event, then counts are normalized over all technique incidences in the corpus. For predicted and reference distributions $p$ and $q$ over their joint technique vocabulary $\mathcal V$, we use
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{TechSim}(p,q)=1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t\in\mathcal V}|p(t)-q(t)|.
\label{eq:technique-similarity}
\end{equation}
The score lies in $[0,1]$, with higher values indicating closer distributions. This metric excludes notation-state markers such as harmonic-region boundaries. It does not evaluate event order, and identical distributions can assign techniques to different events; ornament F1 retains a local comparison. Tone-type distribution similarity uses the same formula over open, stopped, and harmonic notes and is reported in the appendix.

Rule Violation Rate measures the fraction of evaluated work units whose final plans contain at least one runtime-detected rule violation. We replay complete scores through the same rule checker, including for conditions that hide diagnostic feedback during inference; a final runtime warning is the operational indicator of a detected violation. Each work unit contributes at most one to the numerator, regardless of how many rules it violates. The denominators are 1,031 work units for the traditional test and 91 for the modern test in the complete-coverage comparisons. We separately report the total number of detected violations. Thus this metric measures final-plan rule compliance, not the frequency of warning messages emitted during inference. It covers only the implemented checks: unresolved expressions are not automatically violations, and absence of detected violations does not certify playability or musical quality.

\paragraph{Models and Comparisons.}
The feedback comparison uses Qwen3.5-9B with the same project-trained two-stage LoRA weights in both conditions. The full system exposes warnings, diagnostic hints, and warning-triggered follow-up interactions. The diagnostic-feedback-off condition hides this feedback and disables its follow-up mechanism while retaining structural validation and pitch candidates. Its public user and tool messages were checked for diagnostic-feedback leakage.

\system{}, single-stage is our trajectory-trained variant without the Fingering--Guqinizer decomposition. It uses a separately trained Qwen3.5-9B LoRA and generates final jianzipu through the same public tools and diagnostic feedback as the full system. It is allowed up to 16 rounds per attempt, while the full pipeline allows eight rounds per stage attempt and up to three Guqinizer passes. Retries and additional passes make their aggregate budgets different; this is a comparison of the two deployed pipelines. Both use complete model-generated histories and the same scoring rules. In Table~\ref{tab:main-results}, our single-stage and feedback-off variants are grouped below the fixed-workflow comparison; Ours highlights the full two-stage configuration.

\paragraph{Direct final-artifact supervision.}
We additionally train a Qwen3.5-9B Direct SFT comparison using the same 3,976 training examples and final committed plans as our single-stage trajectory supervision. Each example retains the initial public musical context and one assistant JSON target, without intermediate reasoning, tool calls, or tool observations. Inference generates one final plan per work unit without tools or diagnostic revision, using the model's own successful preceding outputs as history. This comparison evaluates the end-to-end artifact-only workflow against trajectory-trained tool interaction; it changes both supervision and inference access to tools. Training is complete; evaluation results remain pending.

We also evaluated the original Qwen3.5-9B checkpoint without the project LoRA using the two-stage agent and the same tool definitions. All 59 attempted inputs matched the test-input hashes. The original-model run allowed 8,192 output tokens per request, eight rounds per stage attempt, up to three attempts, and up to three Guqinizer passes, with a context limit of 16,384 tokens. After a work unit failed, the evaluator skipped the remainder of that score, preserving the requirement that history contain model-generated predecessors. These limits match the current SFT evaluation entry point.

The commercial-model ReAct + Handbook baseline uses GLM-5.3 through its API with the production single-stage prompt and a fixed 73-entry technique handbook. It allows eight rounds and two attempts at temperature 0.25, uses the same tools and scoring pipeline, and produces protocol-valid outputs for all 1,031 units. A separate GLM API baseline with prompts optimized by GEPA on development data remains pending \cite{agrawal2026gepa}. These comparisons involve different model backends and do not isolate prompt optimization from model capability. GEPA's reflection model, API settings, optimization budget, and inference costs will be reported. Direct generation with the original Qwen checkpoint remains an optional additional comparison.

\subsection{Main Results}

\paragraph{Original-model completion and protocol failures.}
The original-model agent attempted 59 of 1,031 work units across the 48 scores. Eleven attempts produced protocol-valid outputs (18.64\% of attempted units), while 48 failed. The remaining 972 units were skipped because a predecessor failed. Valid-output coverage was therefore 11/1,031 (1.07\%); skipped units are excluded from the attempted-unit protocol-failure denominator. All three adapted conditions produced valid outputs for all 1,031 units. We do not report full-test musical metrics for the original model or compare its 11 successful units directly with the adapted model's complete-test scores.

Of the 48 failed units, 13 failed during Fingering and 35 during Guqinizer. Logs record output truncation in 45 failed units, at least one attempt reaching round eight in 26, and three consecutive pitch-candidate-query rounds in 31. These observations overlap and are not a mutually exclusive attribution of failures. They suggest that sustained tool interaction and termination behavior are substantial obstacles for the original model under the current protocol, alongside the musical decisions required by the task.

\paragraph{Technique distributions and the commercial-model baseline.}
Technique distribution similarity is 0.7618 for single-stage SFT, 0.7616 for two-stage feedback OFF, and 0.7301 for the two-stage full system. Thus, fewer runtime warnings do not imply closer aggregate technique usage. GLM-5.3 with ReAct + Handbook attains 99.760\% pitch accuracy and 2.542 cents MAE, with 102 final warnings across 52 units (5.044\%). Its technique similarity is 0.6323 and ornament F1 is 7.158, below both trained students. Its tone-type distribution similarity is higher, however (Appendix~\ref{sec:detailed-results}). These measures capture different aspects of realization; the cross-model comparison alone does not establish that trajectory training is necessary for idiomatic playing.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}\label{sec:ablations}

The full system attains 99.981\% pitch accuracy on 15,758 evaluable events, compared with 86.237\% on 15,360 events when diagnostic feedback is disabled (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). \paragraph{Residual warnings and error analysis.}
The full system leaves two final runtime warnings in two work units, compared with 3,436 warnings across 809 work units for OFF. The rule violation rate is 0.194\% versus 78.468\%. One remaining ON warning in \emph{Youlan} is an octave error, realizing E6 for a target E7. These cases warrant inspection of inherited state, compound notation, and ordinary pitch choices. Final-warning counts and pitch-error counts use different eligibility and matching rules and should not be treated as interchangeable metrics.

\paragraph{Fingering agreement and idiomatic realization.}
The full system's string--hui, left-hand, right-hand, and tone-type F1 scores are 35.444, 57.119, 46.009, and 62.543, respectively. Their modest differences from OFF accompany a much larger pitch-accuracy gap. The full system also has lower ornament F1: 17.013 versus 18.339. The current comparison provides evidence of improved pitch consistency without establishing an advantage in ornament selection.

Tone distributions indicate that pitch accuracy leaves room for different realization strategies. Among recognized tone types on reference-labeled events, harmonics account for 7.51\% of ON predictions, compared with 29.34\% of reference tone types. A system can realize the correct pitch through different strings, positions, or tone types, and can also underuse stylistically appropriate techniques. Both possibilities motivate examining action distributions and consulting performers when assessing idiomatic quality.

\paragraph{Teacher trajectory construction.}
We compare our single-stage variant with a student trained on fixed-workflow trajectories. Each fixed trajectory contains generic reasoning, one real pitch-candidate query, and one edit constructed from the original expert annotation, without a teacher API rollout. Our teacher trajectories instead supervise the teacher's final committed plans after interaction. Both students are evaluated with diagnostic feedback and a 16-round stage limit on the same 48 scores using complete generated histories. The recipes differ in interaction traces, final targets, and some system/user prompts; this comparison evaluates the complete construction recipes rather than isolating reasoning supervision.

The fixed-workflow student achieves 100\% protocol-valid coverage, but pitch accuracy is 26.778\% on 13,556 evaluable events and MAE is 517.624 cents. On their 12,792 common evaluable events, accuracy is 26.931\% versus 98.781\% (Table~\ref{tab:trajectory-ablation}). \paragraph{Inference cost.}\label{sec:inference-cost}
We distinguish recorded token usage from a reproducible API-equivalent cost estimate. For input tokens $I$, cache-hit input tokens $H$ (a subset of $I$), and output tokens $O$, the token charge is
\begin{equation}
 C=\bigl[r_{\mathrm{in}}(I-H)+r_{\mathrm{cache}}H+r_{\mathrm{out}}O\bigr]/10^6,
\end{equation}
where rates are USD per million tokens; cache creation and storage are additional when applicable. We use Together AI's public service prices checked on November 2, 2026: Qwen3.5-9B input/output rates are \$0.17/\$0.25, and GLM-5.3 input/cached-input/output rates are \$1.40/\$0.26/\$4.40.\footnote{\url{https://www.together.ai/pricing}; GLM rates also match \url{https://docs.z.ai/guides/overview/pricing}. These are provider list prices, not a manufacturer price for self-hosted adapters.}
Figure~\ref{fig:quality-tradeoff} uses model-specific public tokenizers and chat templates to retokenize the visible requests and responses retained with each final output. Qwen requests are saved verbatim; GLM inputs are reconstructed from saved user prompts and tool receipts with the production system prompt and fixed handbook. Server-side cache-hit counts were not retained, so we use an explicit no-cache-discount scenario ($H=0$), not a claim of measured zero cache hits. These estimates exclude discarded runs, training, external tool execution, and unrecorded hidden reasoning or provider-only framing. Failed calls whose response usage was not saved are also excluded, not assigned zero cost; preflight context rejections are tracked separately. They describe retained visible-trajectory workload at public API rates, not complete historical usage, actual invoices, or local GPU expenses.

Of 1,528 events with a nonempty target pitch, 1,334 are auditor-resolvable for the two-stage system and 1,402 for the single-stage system, corresponding to 87.304\% and 91.754\% coverage. Conditional PitchAcc@50c is 98.951\% and 97.932\%, respectively; the proportion of all 1,528 target events both resolved and correct is 86.387\% and 89.856\%. On the 1,248 events resolvable for both, the corresponding accuracies are 99.920\% and 98.157\%. Full-score runtime replay yields 15 warnings across three work units for two-stage, versus 42 across 17 for single-stage. These are rule-based diagnostics, not independent performer judgments; the stage and retry budgets also differ between methods.

ReAct + Handbook attains 97.699\% pitch accuracy on 1,347 auditor-resolvable events, with 16.035 cents MAE and 33 warnings across five units. Its string--hui F1, technique similarity, and ornament F1 are 36.627, 0.7118, and 39.961, respectively. As on traditional scores, the single-stage student's reference agreement can exceed that of the two-stage system despite lower conditional pitch accuracy. Disabling diagnostic feedback yields 73.485\% pitch accuracy on 1,403 evaluable events and 472 final warnings across 78 units. On the 1,271 events resolvable for both ON and OFF, accuracy is 98.899\% versus 73.879\%. OFF's technique similarity (0.8623) exceeds the full system's, again showing that aggregate reference usage and pitch consistency capture different properties.

The fixed-workflow student obtains 19.298\% pitch accuracy on 1,197 evaluable events, with 558.105 cents MAE and 1,060 warnings across 88 units. On the 1,119 events resolvable for both single-stage students, the teacher-trajectory student attains 98.749\% versus 19.929\% for fixed workflow. Thus, the large construction-recipe gap also occurs on modern melodies and persists on shared events. Prompt and training-trajectory differences still limit causal attribution to any individual construction step.

\subsection{Expert Evaluation}
A blinded performer study is planned with one or two guqin experts who will actually play selected scores. Each will rate two dimensions on a four-point scale: \emph{performance feasibility}, covering string and fingering choices and continuity, and \emph{idiomatic and overall musical expression}, covering the suitability of techniques and audible \emph{yunwei}. Scale anchors and sampling will be fixed before assessment. Examples will be anonymized and presented in randomized order, with input scores available to raters. We will report rater experience and sampling, and inter-rater agreement if two experts assess the same examples. Expert scores and claims of superior playability or expression await this assessment.
