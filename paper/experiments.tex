\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Data and models.}
We train on 153 scores (3,976 work units) and evaluate on 48 traditional scores (1,031 units) and 10 modern melodies (91 units). Splits are defined at score level. Each method carries its own generated history across work units, and reference jianzipu is reserved for offline scoring. All students use Qwen3.5-9B with 4-bit LoRA. The two-stage adapter learns jointly from 7,952 stage-specific conversations. Each single-stage recipe uses 3,976 examples. Appendix~\ref{sec:reproducibility} specifies training and inference budgets.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
We compare with the original Qwen checkpoint in the two-stage agent, GLM-5.3 ReAct and GEPA with the same technique handbook, and Direct SFT. GEPA results are pending \cite{agrawal2026gepa}. Direct SFT learns the same final teacher plans as our single-stage student and generates once per unit without tools or repair. We also compare fixed-workflow supervision and disable diagnostic feedback in the two-stage system.

\paragraph{Inference protocol.}
Tool-based Qwen runs permit up to three attempts, with 16 rounds per single-stage attempt or eight rounds per stage attempt in the two-stage system. Feedback ON allows up to three Guqinizer passes, while OFF uses one. GLM ReAct allows eight rounds per attempt and two attempts. These ceilings define the deployed pipelines and can yield different total interaction budgets.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
PitchAcc@50c measures pitch fidelity on auditor-resolvable events. We also compare methods on shared resolvable events. Position F1 compares exact string--hui pairs, and ornament F1 compares ornament labels, while Tech. Sim. and Tone Sim. compare distributions. Diversity-adjusted Nonbasic Rate discounts the rate of rule-compliant nonbasic technique use by within-score technique repetition (Appendix~\ref{sec:technique-breadth}). Rule Violation Rate counts audited units with at least one final violation. Valid-output coverage uses all test units as its denominator. Musical metrics for incomplete runs apply to valid contiguous prefixes. Appendix~\ref{sec:evaluation-details} specifies the scoring rules.

On traditional scores, both \system variants complete all 1,031 work units. The two-stage system achieves 99.981\% conditional pitch accuracy and a 0.194\% rule violation rate, compared with 99.760\% and 5.044\% for GLM-5.3 ReAct with the handbook (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}). Both variants also achieve higher Position F1 and ornament F1 than ReAct on these scores, while ReAct more closely matches the reference tone distribution. Rule violations matter because a pitch-correct plan can still disrupt how a phrase is played or sounds. For example, stopping an open string too soon can cut off its resonance, a slide with the wrong direction can change the intended contour, and a difficult same-finger shift can interrupt continuity. The violation rate therefore captures departures from encoded guqin playing practices that pitch accuracy and reference-action agreement alone can miss.

Direct SFT yields 85.277\% pitch accuracy on its valid traditional prefixes, with stronger ornament agreement than either \system variant. On modern melodies, where Direct has full protocol coverage, its pitch accuracy and rule consistency still trail both variants. The comparison spans both supervision and tool access. The original Qwen agent produces valid outputs for 11 of 1,031 traditional units. Appendix~\ref{sec:supplementary-experiments} reports its failure diagnostics, and Appendix~\ref{sec:direct-results} details Direct's coverage and shared-event comparisons.

The baselines also expose a distinction between reproducing reference gestures and realizing the melody. On modern melodies, Direct SFT nearly matches the single-stage student's ornament F1 (43.437 vs. 43.445), yet on the 1,315 events resolved for both, its pitch accuracy is 80.760\% versus 98.023\%. This gap persists on a common event set even though the methods differ in which outputs the auditor can resolve.

\paragraph{What does expert guidance add to a fixed workflow?}
Both single-stage students can query pitch candidates and edit the plan at inference. Their training differs in what those interactions demonstrate. Fixed-workflow trajectories contain one scripted query followed by an edit derived from the original annotation. Expert-guided trajectories demonstrate how to select, check, and revise an executable plan. On 12,792 shared traditional events, pitch accuracy rises from 26.931\% to 98.781\%, even though the fixed-workflow model has higher ornament F1. The gap persists on the modern set (19.929\% vs. 98.749\% on 1,119 shared events). These comparisons support the expert-guided recipe as a whole. Its trajectories, final targets, and some prompts differ from the scripted recipe.

\paragraph{What does two-stage planning trade off?}
The two-stage system improves pitch accuracy on shared traditional events and leaves violations in only 2 of 1,031 units, compared with 147 for single-stage (Table~\ref{tab:ablation-summary}). The single-stage student more closely reproduces reference actions, including string--hui positions and ornaments. One illustration of why pitch accuracy leaves room for different performance choices is tone type. Among recognized tone types on reference-labeled events, harmonics make up 7.51\% of the full system's predictions versus 29.34\% in the reference. That difference matters when interpreting near-perfect pitch accuracy. The two variants use separately trained adapters and different interaction budgets, so the comparison evaluates the complete pipelines.

\paragraph{What does diagnostic feedback contribute?}
Holding the two-stage adapter fixed, enabling diagnostics and their follow-up passes improves accuracy by 13.038 percentage points on 14,289 shared traditional events. The modern set shows the same pattern (98.899\% vs. 73.879\% on 1,271 shared events). Both settings retain pitch queries and structural checks, and the same final rules score their outputs. On the traditional set, final warnings fall from 3,436 to 2. One residual case in \emph{Youlan} realizes E6 for a target E7, illustrating that a bounded revision loop can still leave a pitch error. This comparison estimates the contribution of the feedback mechanism, including the extra revision opportunities it enables. Appendix~\ref{sec:supplementary-experiments} reports full counts and reference-agreement diagnostics.

All six completed methods cover all 91 modern work units. The two-stage pipeline is more accurate on its resolvable events (98.951\% vs. 97.932\%) and on the 1,248 events both variants resolve (99.920\% vs. 98.157\%). Single-stage resolves 1,402 of the 1,528 target events, compared with 1,334 for two-stage. Consequently, the share of all targets that the auditor can verify as correct favors single-stage (89.856\% vs. 86.387\%). Reporting both measures distinguishes errors on resolved notes from differences in what the auditor can verify. The independence of these melodies from all development procedures remains to be verified.

Figure~\ref{fig:quality-tradeoff} compares pitch accuracy with diversity-adjusted nonbasic technique use and with estimated inference cost. On the modern set, the full system costs an estimated \$0.824 per 100 attempted work units, compared with \$1.770 for single-stage and \$7.012 for GLM ReAct with the handbook. The two-stage feedback-off variant costs \$0.370, showing the added workload associated with feedback and follow-up revision alongside the gains in shared-event accuracy. Direct SFT has the lowest estimated cost (\$0.027) and lower pitch accuracy than either trajectory-trained variant. These estimates price retained visible requests and responses at public API rates without cache discounts. Appendix~\ref{sec:inference-cost} specifies the accounting scope, and Appendix~\ref{sec:supplementary-experiments} reports the remaining modern-melody diagnostics.
